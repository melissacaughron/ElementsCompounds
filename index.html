🧪 Elements & Compounds Practice

Compare and contrast elements and compounds using atoms and molecules, chemical symbols, and chemical formulas.

Key ideas

ElementCompound
Made ofOnly one kind of atomTwo or more kinds of atoms chemically bonded
SymbolHas its own symbol (Na, O, Fe)No single symbol; uses a formula (H2O, NaCl)
FormulaOften just the symbol (Fe, He) or a molecule like O2Shows which elements and how many atoms of each (CO2)
Atoms & moleculesAtoms of the same kind; may exist as molecules (O2, N2)Atoms of different kinds bonded together (many are molecules, like H2O)
Broken down?Not by chemical changesYes, into simpler substances by chemical changes

Vocabulary

  • Atom: the smallest unit of an element.
  • Molecule: two or more atoms bonded together. A molecule can be an element (O2) or a compound (H2O).
  • Chemical symbol: one or two letters. The first is capital, the second is lowercase (Cl, not CL). Co is cobalt, but CO is carbon monoxide!
  • Chemical formula: symbols plus subscripts (small numbers) that tell how many atoms of each element. No number means 1.

Compounds can have properties very different from the elements in them. Sodium is a reactive metal and chlorine is a poisonous gas, but together they make table salt.
